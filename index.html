01

Why Engineering

ON WHY I DO THIS

Engineering, to me, is the discipline of turning "I don't know if this is possible" into a working prototype. That question is what pulled me toward math early on, not because I was chasing grades, but because a good equation is the closest thing to a guarantee you get in an uncertain world. The further I pushed into harder problems, the more I wanted a career spent testing the edges of what's already been built, whether that's a satellite headed for orbit or a machine nobody has designed yet.

Aerospace pulls at that same instinct. It's one of the few fields where every gram, every tolerance, and every material choice gets checked against physics with zero room to bluff. That mix of rigor and the unknown, of knowing the math has to hold up before anything leaves the ground, is what keeps me building.

02

Why Me

A SHORT VERSION OF MY COVER LETTER

I take problems apart before I trust them. Before a design is anything more than a sketch, I want to know its tolerances, its materials, and how it actually gets manufactured, not just what it looks like rendered. Leading the CalgaryToSpace structural team taught me how to hold a design to a standard someone else set, ExoLaunch's launch regulations, coordinate ten people who each own a piece of it, and still be the one troubleshooting a fixture the night before a shipment.

I pick up new tools quickly because I've had to: CAD, CNC programming, and circuit simulation were all things I learned on the job because a project needed them. I'd rather ask a clarifying question early than guess and rebuild later. What I'm looking for is a team building something real, where I can bring that mix of structural rigor and hands-on manufacturing sense, and leave having learned how the professionals do it.
